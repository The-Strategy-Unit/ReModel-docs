How ReModel works

ReModel will run in 2 monthly cycles, with each cycle focusing on a different simulation model. Each cycle will consist of two sessions: a Running session and a Reusing session.

Session 1: Running - this session will be an opportunity to clone and run the model, reproduce an existing result or scenario, and confidently explore the model and its outputs. Participants will be encouraged to ask questions and share their insights.

Session 2: Reusing - this session will focus on the technical aspects of the model, including its structure, implementation, data requirements and input assumptions. Participants will consider what they would change, whether they understand the model well enough to make those changes, and how an edited model should be checked and documented.

Each session will last for 1 hour, and will follow a participative discussion format.

The sessions are free to attend, but spaces will be capped at 15 places per session on a first-come, first-served basis; NHS analysts will receive priority.

Register for ReModel

You can sign up for one, several or all ReModel cycles. Each cycle focuses on one open simulation model and includes two linked sessions: Running and Reusing. Registration for a cycle covers both sessions in that cycle.

Cycle 1: Non-Elective Flow Simulation

Cycle 1 focuses on the open-source Non-Elective Flow Simulation, a discrete-event simulation of patients moving from the emergency department into same-day emergency care or an inpatient bed. We will explore how bed capacity, length of stay and same-day emergency care activity affect queues and waiting times.

Cycle 2: Meeting the demand of NHS 111 for primary care services

Cycle 2 focuses on MOOOD, an open-source discrete-event simulation that follows callers after an NHS 111 primary-care disposition and models their subsequent contact with primary care, NHS 111, ambulance services and emergency departments. We will explore how timely primary-care contact affects activity across services and potentially avoidable emergency department attendance.

Cycle 3: The Value of Triage during Periods of Intense COVID-19 Demand

Cycle 3 focuses on an open R simulation of intensive-care triage during periods of intense COVID-19 demand. We will explore how first-come, first-served admission, demand-based triage and reverse triage interact with demand, length of stay and capacity, and affect lives and life-years lost.

Code of Conduct

Community rules:

  1. Be respectful: Treat all participants with respect and kindness. Harassment, discrimination, or any form of abusive behavior will not be tolerated.
  2. Camera and microphone etiquette: When participating in video calls, ensure your camera and microphone are used appropriately. Mute your microphone when not speaking to minimize background noise. We ask that you keep your camera on during sessions to foster a more engaging and interactive environment, but we understand that there may be circumstances where this is not possible.
  3. Stay on topic: Keep discussions relevant to the purpose of the community. Off-topic conversations should be taken to private messages.
  4. Share knowledge: Contribute your expertise and insights to help others. Be open to learning from others as well.
  5. Report issues: If you encounter any problems or witness rule violations, report them to the moderators.
